G-01

Paper cup sizes: which size suits your café?

Paper cups are usually sized in fluid ounces (oz); one ounce is about 30 ml. Four sizes cover most cafés: 4 oz for espresso, 8 oz for cappuccino and flat white, 12 oz for lattes and regular drinks, and 16 oz for large drinks. Most cafés need only two or three of them.Read the guide →G-02

Offset vs digital printing: the difference and when to use each

Digital printing prints straight from the file with no plates, so it suits short runs, urgent jobs and personalised copies. Offset needs plates and press setup, but the cost per piece falls as quantity rises and colour stays more consistent, so it suits large runs.Read the guide →G-03

How to prepare a print-ready file: a checklist before you send

A print-ready file is usually in CMYK, at 300 dpi at final size, with about 3 mm of bleed on every side and fonts outlined or embedded, exported as a PDF. Special finishes such as foil, spot UV and die-cut lines need their own separate layers.Read the guide →G-04

Print finishes explained: foil, spot UV, lamination and die-cutting

Finishes are steps after printing that protect a piece and lift the brand. Lamination covers the whole surface in gloss, matte or soft-touch; hot foil adds metallic gold, silver or holographic shine; spot UV adds a raised clear gloss to selected elements; and die-cutting gives the piece a custom shape.Read the guide →G-05

Paper types and weights: which paper suits your print?

Paper weight is measured in grams per square metre (gsm). Office paper is around 80 gsm, flyers and brochure pages 115–170 gsm, covers and menus 200–300 gsm, and business cards usually 300–350 gsm. The paper type — coated, uncoated or kraft — shapes the feel and how colour looks as much as the weight does.Read the guide →
