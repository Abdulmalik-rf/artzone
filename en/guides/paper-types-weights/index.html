Paper types and weights: which paper suits your print?

Art Zone Printing prepress team·Updated: 25 October 2026

Paper weight is measured in grams per square metre (gsm). Office paper is around 80 gsm, flyers and brochure pages 115–170 gsm, covers and menus 200–300 gsm, and business cards usually 300–350 gsm. The paper type — coated, uncoated or kraft — shapes the feel and how colour looks as much as the weight does.

What does gsm mean?

The number is the weight of one square metre of the paper. A higher number usually means a heavier, stiffer sheet, but actual thickness varies by type: uncoated paper feels thicker than coated paper of the same gsm.

Common weights and their uses

Approx. weightTypical use
80–120 gsmLetterheads, document pages, notebook inner pages
115–170 gsmFlyers, brochure and catalogue inner pages
200–300 gsmBrochure and catalogue covers, menus, stiff posters
300–350 gsmBusiness cards, invitations, hang tags
Folding boardSmall boxes and product packaging
Corrugated boardShipping and delivery boxes; graded by flute, not just gsm

These are typical market ranges; the final choice depends on use, budget and the feel you want.

Paper types

  • Coated (art) paper: a smooth surface that keeps colours vivid and sharp, in gloss or matte. Best for brochures, catalogues and flyers.
  • Uncoated paper: a natural feel that takes pen easily, so it is used for letterheads, notebooks and forms.
  • Textured linen: an embossed feel that makes business cards and invitations feel premium.
  • Kraft: natural, unbleached brown paper, common for bags, boxes and hang tags when a brand wants a natural look.
  • Board: folding board for small and product boxes, corrugated board for shipping boxes.
  • Vinyl and adhesive stock: for stickers, in clear, white and waterproof versions.

Gloss or matte coated?

Gloss makes photos and colours livelier but reflects light. Matte is easier to read and looks calmer and more refined, so it suits text-heavy pieces.

Tips before you choose

  • Heavier is not always better: heavy stock needs scoring before folding, or it cracks along the fold.
  • Think about use: a menu handled every day needs stronger stock and lamination; a one-off flyer can be lighter.
  • Think about shipping: heavier paper adds weight and cost on large runs.
  • Ask for swatches: Art Zone provides physical material swatches with the quote, so you can feel the paper before printing.

Quick answers

What paper weight suits business cards?

Most commonly 300–350 gsm; premium cards use heavier stock or textured linen.

What is the difference between gloss and matte coated paper?

Gloss brings out colours and photos and reflects light; matte is calmer, easier to read and suits text-heavy pieces.
