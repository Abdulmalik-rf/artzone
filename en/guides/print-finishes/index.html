Print finishes explained: foil, spot UV, lamination and die-cutting

Art Zone Printing prepress team·Updated: 25 October 2026

Finishes are steps after printing that protect a piece and lift the brand. Lamination covers the whole surface in gloss, matte or soft-touch; hot foil adds metallic gold, silver or holographic shine; spot UV adds a raised clear gloss to selected elements; and die-cutting gives the piece a custom shape.

Finishes at a glance

FinishWhat it doesBest for
Gloss laminationClear glossy film that protects and makes colour popMenus, covers, heavily handled print
Matte laminationNon-reflective film with a calm, refined lookBrochures, business cards, covers
Soft-touch laminationVelvety feel in the handPremium business cards and gift boxes
Hot foilMetallic foil fixed with a heated dieLogos and titles on cards and boxes
Spot UVRaised clear gloss on selected elementsA logo shining over a matte or soft-touch surface
Die-cuttingCuts the piece to a custom shape with a dieBoxes, shaped stickers, cards with special edges

Lamination: gloss, matte or soft-touch?

Lamination is a thin film bonded over the whole printed surface; it protects against moisture and scuffs and makes the piece last longer. Gloss makes colours pop and reflects light, matte is calmer and easier to read under strong light, and soft-touch gives a velvety feel customers notice the moment they pick up the card or box.

Dark soft-touch surfaces can show fingerprints and scuffs with heavy handling, so they suit premium pieces better than print that is passed around all day.

Hot foil

A thin metallic foil is transferred onto the paper with a heated die, giving gold, silver or holographic shine that ordinary inks cannot. It is typically used for logos, titles and security seals.

  • Prepare foil elements as vector artwork, not images.
  • Avoid very fine lines and tiny text; foil may not hold cleanly in them.
  • Put foil elements on their own layer in a single colour.

Spot UV

A clear gloss coating applied only to chosen parts of the design and cured with ultraviolet light, so it feels slightly raised. It looks best over matte or soft-touch lamination: the contrast between gloss and the calm surface is what catches the eye.

Die-cutting

A die is made in the outline of the shape to cut and crease the piece precisely. It is the basis of boxes, shaped stickers and cards with custom shapes. For stickers, a kiss-cut cuts through the sticker but not the backing, so it peels off easily.

  • Draw the cut line on its own layer in a spot colour; it is not printed.
  • Leave bleed around the cut line and keep text away from it.
  • For boxes, ask for the template (die-line) for your size before designing.

Popular combinations

  • Premium business card: soft-touch + spot UV on the logo + gold foil on the name.
  • Gift box: matte lamination + gold foil logo + die-cut.
  • Restaurant menu: gloss lamination to survive daily handling.

Each finish adds a production step, which affects cost and lead time depending on quantity. For file preparation in detail, see the guide to preparing print-ready files.

Quick answers

Can foil and spot UV go on the same card?

Yes. It is one of the most popular premium combinations, as long as each finish has its own layer in the file.

What is the difference between spot UV and gloss lamination?

Gloss lamination covers the whole surface; spot UV is applied only to chosen elements and is slightly raised.

Do finishes add to the lead time?

Yes, each finish is an extra production step. We confirm the exact date with the quote, based on quantity and finish.
